How Millersville Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Common Water Heater Problems in Millersville

Around Millersville, water heater trouble usually come from a handful of sources. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, insulating the water from the burner, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling you may hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater on hard water needs annual descaling. And an older installation getting a new heater often needs code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. All of these is fixable, and most are much cheaper to address early.
